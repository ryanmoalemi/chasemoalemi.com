Work

Auto parts are a data business

A customer isn't buying brake pads. They're buying the brake pads that fit their car. I use AI on the inventory, the catalog and the fitment data.

  • Fitment is complicated

    Year, make and model isn't enough. Engine, trim and options decide what fits.

  • Part numbers change

    Old numbers get replaced, but buyers still search them. The catalog has to know both.

  • Suppliers don't agree

    Every supplier describes the same part in its own format. The catalog has to turn those into one record.

  • AI as a second reader

    A second read can catch a conflict, like a 4WD title on a 2WD part, before a customer does.

  • Where AI stops

    AI can check fitment. It shouldn't decide it. The manufacturer's data is the source of truth.

  • Returns are data

    A wrong-part return points straight at the listing that caused it. It's the cheapest audit there is.

Hobbies

Cards as an alternative asset

I collect Pokémon and sports cards the way an investor looks at any market: supply, demand, and what things actually sell for.

  • Sealed vs. singles

    A sealed box gets scarcer every time someone else opens one. A single is priced by how many people want that exact card.

  • Read the pop report

    It shows graded supply, not total supply. The number I watch is how many were submitted.

  • Comps over asks

    Sold prices tell you the market. Asking prices tell you what someone hopes for.

  • Two different markets

    Pokémon runs on nostalgia and artwork. Sports cards follow players and the news.

  • Sports

    I follow all of them, all year. There's usually a game on, and I usually have an opinion about it.
